# A check without history counts against the rating and the snowflake

The Rating is the share of Holds among all published Checks, and each Snowflake axis is the share of Holds among all the Checks on it; a Check whose verdict is "needs more history" scores zero in both and is drawn hollow, and no company is rated A while any Check lacks history. We chose this over scoring Holds among evaluated Checks because the reader cannot see what the engine did not evaluate: in November 2026 two lenders passed five of five evaluated Checks and showed an A on five of eight, while the three unevaluated Checks were the dividend and promoter ones. The cost is that thin data now reads as a low letter and a small shape, which is the honest reading; the chip and caption state the coverage ("6 of 10 hold, 9 evaluated") so a reader can tell thin data from bad data. Every PE the engine compares is priced on trailing four-quarter EPS where four consecutive quarters exist, for the same reason: an annualized monsoon or festival quarter is not a year.
